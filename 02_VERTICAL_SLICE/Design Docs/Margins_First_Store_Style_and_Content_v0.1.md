# Margins First-Store Style and Content v0.1

## Status and authority

- **Status:** Proposed for project-owner review
- **Approved foundation applied:** Stylized Contemporary Americana; grounded tone with light humor
- **Scope:** Graybox dimensions, first-store identity, asset priorities, presentation standards, and non-goals
- **Asset status:** No asset, license, generated output, brand, scene, material, or runtime content is approved by this sheet

## Store purpose

The first store should feel like a plausible small leased neighborhood convenience store that has been stripped back for a new operator. The initial emptiness makes growth physical: fixtures, stock, signs, cleanliness, and lighting visibly convert a bare commercial unit into a functioning business.

|---|---:|---:|---|

These ranges are not construction documents or legal accessibility claims. Unity playtesting and navigation measurements must determine the accepted values.

## Functional layout zones

1. **Threshold and storefront:** entry, open/closed sign, visual read of the store.
2. **Checkout zone:** counter, scanner, staged transaction fixture, impulse shelf opportunity later.
3. **Primary sales floor:** one wall shelf and one short gondola or equivalent essential shelf footprint.
4. **Backroom:** delivery drop zone, stock rack, loose-product recovery point.
5. **Task zone:** mop bucket, spill location, or one basic maintenance point.
6. **Circulation spine:** unobstructed route linking entry, checkout, shelves, backroom, and task.

Fixture silhouettes and floor treatment should make each zone understandable before final signage or UI exists.

## Storefront identity

Proposed direction:

- modest leased strip-center or small standalone infill unit;
- broad glass frontage broken into simplified panels;
- one strong rectangular sign band with an original placeholder store identity;
- slightly faded neutral exterior shell contrasted by the player’s cleaner sign and interior glow;
- practical delivery access at the side or rear;
- no fuel canopy, pumps, car wash, or gas-station branding.

The store should feel contemporary and American without tying it to a real chain, city, or trademark.

## Interior identity

- Warm neutral walls and ceiling establish an inexpensive leased shell.
- Fixtures use dark charcoal or muted painted metal with light wood or warm laminate accents.
- Product packaging provides controlled color clusters.
- Checkout is the strongest functional silhouette near the entrance.
- Backroom materials are simpler, cooler, and less finished than the sales floor.
- Cleanliness changes should be readable through localized roughness, decal, color, or prop state when production art exists.
- Humor belongs in original fictional labels and signs, not exaggerated geometry that undermines business credibility.

## Stylized Contemporary Americana application

Apply the approved reference responsibilities through original work:

- use simplified, bold silhouettes and selective detail rather than photoreal clutter;
- use authored roadside-commercial color and signage rhythms without copying any protected storefront;
- keep character and object interaction complexity achievable at the practical approved reference level;
- use warm/cool lighting contrast, graphic shadows, and controlled atmosphere to shape the small space;
- preserve believable shelf depth, counter height, box scale, aisle width, and hand reach.

Named references define selected responsibilities only. No protected design, logo, layout, character, texture, or brand may be copied.

## Shape language

- Building shell: broad rectangles with a few softened corners.
- Fixtures: sturdy rectangular frames, inset shelves, visible feet, chunky handles.
- Checkout equipment: compact wedges and rounded rectangles to distinguish interactive tools.
- Delivery boxes: slightly softened cuboids with large graphic labels and one readable opening seam.
- Cleaning equipment: tall simple handle plus broad bucket silhouette.
- Products: category-specific silhouettes at readable scale, not identical cubes with different textures.
- Interaction affordances: repeated accent-color strips, handles, or inset panels rather than floating emissive outlines everywhere.

## Material simplification

Limit first-pass materials to a small authored family:

- painted drywall;
- sealed concrete or simple commercial tile;
- painted metal;
- warm laminate or inexpensive composite counter;
- clear storefront glass;
- corrugated cardboard;
- simplified plastic, paper, foil, and aluminum packaging groups.

Use consistent roughness and broad color blocking. Avoid unique high-resolution materials for every asset during the first proof.

## Lighting and color goals

- Neutral daylight or cool exterior light establishes the bare shell.
- Warm practical fixtures make the opened store feel active.
- Checkout and primary shelf receive the clearest light hierarchy.
- Backroom remains readable without competing with the sales floor.
- Valid and invalid interaction color is not part of the permanent palette and must have shape/text alternatives.
- Opening and closing may later shift sign and practical-light states; no day/night system is required now.
- Avoid crushed blacks, excessive bloom, chromatic aberration, or dramatic exposure changes that obscure products and prompts.

## Signage philosophy

- Signs communicate function first and personality second.
- Store identity, open/closed state, category markers, prices, delivery labels, and safety/task cues form one coherent family.
- Use original fictional names and copy approved separately by the project owner.
- Large words and simple icons should remain readable at first-person distances.
- Avoid dense parody text, real chain resemblance, trademark-shaped color blocking, and walls covered in decorative signs.

## Product-packaging abstraction

- Use six proposed starter products across beverage, snack, candy, grocery, and household categories.
- Each category needs a distinct silhouette or primary color family.
- Labels should use large original brand blocks and short readable product names.
- Small legal copy, barcodes, nutrition panels, and photoreal food imagery are unnecessary for the proof.
- Price and product identity must remain available through prompts or checkout information rather than requiring label reading.
- Packaging variants should share meshes and material layouts when practical.

## Fixture and equipment hierarchy

| Priority | Asset | Reason |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Checkout counter and scanner | Defines opening and sale completion |
| 1 | One modular shelf family | Core stocking and capacity proof |
| 1 | Delivery box and drop zone | Receiving and inventory transfer |
| 1 | Backroom stock rack | Loose inventory location |
| 1 | Cleaning task prop | Required operating task |

| 2 | Entry door and storefront glass | Threshold and store identity |
| 2 | Price/category label system | Product readability |
| 3 | Trash bin, mat, small counter props | Lived-in credibility after loop works |

## Asset reuse strategy

- One shelf frame supports wall and gondola variants through widths, end panels, and shelf counts.
- Checkout counter uses the same laminate and metal family as fixtures.
- Box mesh and label layout support multiple products.
- Product meshes reuse category families rather than one mesh for everything.
- Store shell modules should later support a second location through dimensions, facade panels, palette, wear, and signage changes.
- Source, license, attribution, modifications, and AI involvement must be recorded before any asset is accepted.

## Animation expectations

First proof:

- deterministic transform movement for held products and fixture previews;
- simple box-open state;
- concise scanner response;
- cleaning progress state change;
- open/closed sign or light state;
- no character hand rig required.

Later consideration:

- authored first-person hand interaction only if it materially improves feel and fits the production budget;
- employee/customer animation only after their systems are in scope.

Avoid long canned animations that lock controls or make repeated work slower.

## Audio priorities

No final audio is implemented in this package. Priority order when audio work is authorized:

1. accepted/rejected interaction cues;
2. product pickup, placement, and box opening;
3. scanner confirmation and correction;
4. cleaning progress and completion;
5. open/close state;
6. restrained store ambience and equipment hum.

Functional cues require visual equivalents. Music, voices, crowds, city ambience, and a complete mix are deferred.

## Explicit visual non-goals

- Photorealism
- Generic asset-pack low-poly presentation
- Protected chain-store imitation
- Fuel pumps or gas-station identity
- Dense fully stocked store before player setup
- Unique meshes and materials for dozens of products
- Final characters, crowds, traffic, weather, or exterior district polish
- Complex shaders, custom rendering architecture, or cinematic post-processing
- Physics-driven loose clutter as core interaction
- Final UI style or framework
- Marketing-ready screenshots

## Review criteria

- The empty-to-operating transformation is visible.
- Every required interaction object has a distinct silhouette.
- Store dimensions support first-person reach and later navigation testing.
- Products remain legible without detailed label reading.
- The scene reads as original contemporary American retail without copying a real or fictional protected store.
- Asset priorities fit the first-store loop and do not pre-produce deferred systems.
