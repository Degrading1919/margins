# Margins Economy and Progression Direction

## Status and authority

- **Status:** Current pre-production economy direction synchronized to the approved foundational decisions
- **Authority:** This document records approved philosophy and boundaries. It does not establish balanced numbers, formulas, milestone placement, or final unlock thresholds.

## Economic philosophy

Margins should be understandable, consequential, and challenging without making one early mistake automatically destroy a long save.

The intended default is **challenging but recoverable**:

- cash flow, rent, payroll, inventory, debt, pricing, demand, maintenance, and management quality matter;
- failure should emerge through visible causes;
- players should receive actionable information;
- recovery options should exist before bankruptcy or restart becomes unavoidable; and
- simulation depth should create decisions rather than bookkeeping or paperwork chores.

## Difficulty layers

Four mode purposes are approved, with final labels and values unresolved:

- forgiving growth-focused play;
- the intended challenging-but-recoverable default;
- a harsher simulation with tighter margins and fewer safety nets;
- sandbox experimentation and construction.

These should share a common simulation foundation rather than become separately maintained games.

## Capability-based progression

Margins does not use a traditional level-gated progression system as its primary structure.

Access and opportunity may depend on:

- capital and cash flow;
- creditworthiness and financing terms;
- reputation and operating history;
- employee and management capacity;
- relevant permits or specialized requirements; and
- modest owner expertise or specialization where useful.

Larger opportunities should become available because the company can support them, not merely because an arbitrary experience bar filled.

## Starting conditions

A normal game begins with limited startup capital and a small leased storefront. Exact cash, debt, interest, collateral, rent, and background modifiers remain unresolved.

Starting backgrounds may alter financial circumstances, early opportunities, or minor dialogue without creating separate campaigns.

## Recovery and failure

Potential recovery categories include:

- reducing costs or operating hours;
- changing product mix, pricing, or staffing;
- returning to hands-on work;
- selling inventory, equipment, assets, or property;
- refinancing or restructuring obligations;
- closing an unprofitable location; and
- accepting less favorable financing or ownership terms where later systems support them.

These are design categories rather than approved exact mechanics.

## Corporate finance progression

Approved direction is phased:

1. personal capital and startup debt;
2. commercial loans, credit lines, mortgages, and equipment financing;
3. optional private investors or strategic partners;
4. acquisitions using cash, debt, seller financing, or ownership stakes;
5. public markets and IPO systems deferred unless private-company and acquisition systems later justify them.

Ownership dilution, repayment obligations, collateral, control rights, and investor expectations should create meaningful tradeoffs.

## Competition and acquisitions

The vertical slice requires understandable local competition represented mainly through aggregate market effects.

Long-term direction is a Coffee Inc–style competitor system in which recognizable companies may expand, decline, change market share, merge, or be acquired.

Deep autonomous rival-company AI, detailed transaction structures, and acquisition milestone placement are unresolved and are not vertical-slice commitments.

## Property and portfolio progression

Economic progression eventually connects business operations, financing, property ownership, renovation, development, outside tenants, multiple brands, and holding-company strategy.

The 1.0 minimum commits property ownership and development plus core holding-company progression, but the exact depth and sequencing remain unresolved.

## Administration

Use layered administrative simulation:

- early play shows understandable cash flow, rent, payroll, inventory costs, debt, and simplified taxes;
- permits, inspections, insurance, contracts, and regulatory risks appear where they create meaningful choices;
- routine administration may be delegated to professionals or headquarters departments;
- advanced reporting may expose optional accounting depth;
- the player does not manually complete tax or legal paperwork.

## Endgame

Margins uses a portfolio-legacy model. Potential ambitions include category dominance, franchising, commercial real estate, district redevelopment, or a diversified holding company. A major legacy project may create formal victory while allowing continued play.

Exact victory thresholds and valuation rules remain unresolved.

## Explicitly unresolved

- starting capital, debt, rent, wages, prices, margins, taxes, and interest;
- demand, satisfaction, reputation, credit, and valuation formulas;
- difficulty modifiers and bankruptcy rules;
- progression thresholds and permit requirements;
- investor, collateral, ownership, and control mechanics;
- property and acquisition valuation;
- rival-company behavior and update cadence;
- M&A milestone placement and transaction depth;
- full 1.0 property and headquarters economy;
- endgame thresholds and legacy scoring;
- exploit prevention and balancing evidence.

All numerical recommendations must be treated as hypotheses until validated through simulation, testing, and project-owner approval.
