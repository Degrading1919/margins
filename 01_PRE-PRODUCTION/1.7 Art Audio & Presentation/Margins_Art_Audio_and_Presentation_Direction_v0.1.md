# Margins Art, Audio, and Presentation Direction

## Status and authority

- **Status:** Current pre-production presentation direction synchronized to the approved foundational decisions
- **Authority:** This document records approved art and tone direction plus production boundaries. It does not approve individual assets, licenses, audio identity, technical-art implementation, or final marketing materials.

## Visual identity

Margins uses **Stylized Contemporary Americana**.

Approved reference responsibilities:

- **Road 96:** primary reference for stylized contemporary and roadside Americana;
- **Schedule I:** practical model and animation complexity reference; and
- **Firewatch:** lighting, color, atmosphere, silhouettes, and environmental composition reference.

These games communicate selected qualities only. They are not replication targets and do not authorize imitation of protected expression, assets, branding, characters, code, or content.

## Visual principles

The visual direction should favor:

- simplified, readable 3D forms;
- believable functional scale for aisles, checkout, storage, deliveries, customer movement, and property use;
- deliberate lighting, color, signage, and silhouettes;
- authored district and storefront identity;
- consistency over raw geometric or texture fidelity;
- modular characters, clothing, hair, buildings, props, products, fixtures, and commercial spaces;
- clear visual differences among neglect, maintenance, renovation, prestige, crowding, and financial growth;
- interaction readability in first-person play;
- a production standard realistic for a solo developer with limited 3D-modeling experience and a pre-revenue budget below $1,000.

Photorealism and generic asset-pack low-poly presentation are not targets.

## Tone

The approved tone is **grounded with light humor**.

> The business is real; the people and brands have personality.

Financial pressure, staffing, debt, failure, and business consequences should remain credible. Humor may appear through fictional brands, packaging, advertising, employee and customer behavior, local businesses, and occasional operational chaos.

Broad comedy and heavy corporate satire are not approved as the core tone.

## Character direction boundary

Characters should be intentionally simplified and modular enough to support solo production, variation, and persistent employees without requiring photoreal human creation.

The exact character proportions, facial system, body types, customization depth, animation set, dialogue presentation, and crowd-variation strategy remain unresolved.

## Environment direction boundary

The city is one fictional contemporary American city composed of expanding handcrafted districts built from reusable modules.

The vertical slice requires one compact commercial block and two convenience-store locations with different market conditions. Exact architecture, district palettes, weather, time-of-day presentation, vegetation, vehicles, signage systems, and environmental density remain unresolved.

## Interface and presentation relationship

Art direction should support clear interaction and reporting rather than obscure them. Store operation, management views, financial reports, alerts, property changes, and portfolio growth should share a coherent presentation identity.

Exact HUD, menu, typography, iconography, dashboard, accessibility, and motion-design systems belong to later UX and presentation work.

## Audio status

Audio remains combined under the Art and Presentation Director role for now, but detailed audio direction has not been approved.

Future work may define requirements for:

- store ambience and equipment;
- checkout, stocking, cleaning, maintenance, and construction feedback;
- city and district ambience;
- employee and customer vocal presentation;
- music and tonal identity;
- accessibility and mix requirements.

This list identifies work areas, not approved content scope or an audio style.

## Asset and generative-AI pipeline

Margins uses a controlled hybrid asset pipeline:

- prefer self-created, properly licensed, open-source, Creative Commons, or affordable commercial assets;
- use generative AI extensively for concepts, references, drafts, variations, automation, and bounded production;
- ship generated material only when provenance and tool terms are documented;
- require human review and intentional acceptance for every shipped asset;
- maintain an asset ledger covering source, license, attribution, modifications, and AI involvement;
- quarantine or replace assets whose ownership or usage rights cannot be established;
- avoid direct imitation of living artists or protected franchise styles.

No asset class is automatically approved for generated final content.

## Explicitly unresolved

- detailed art bible and shape language;
- palettes, shaders, materials, textures, and lighting implementation;
- polygon, texture, rig, animation, and performance budgets;
- character creator and customization scope;
- product-packaging and fictional-brand standards;
- city, district, architecture, prop, vehicle, weather, and vegetation standards;
- UI presentation and accessibility requirements;
- audio identity, dialogue, voice, music, and mix scope;
- asset sources and specific licenses;
- marketing-image, trailer, and storefront-art requirements;
- engine-specific art pipeline and tools.

Every asset remains subject to project-owner acceptance, technical feasibility, gameplay readability, provenance, and licensing review.
